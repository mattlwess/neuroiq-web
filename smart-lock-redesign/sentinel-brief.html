The battery and the subscription are the product's two weakest bolts. Pull both.

A facility-wired, battery-free, operator-owned smart lock for self-storage — engineered to design around the Nokē and DaVinci patent walls while folding in Storage Defender–class sensing.

The wedge: Nokē and DaVinci must sell to any facility assuming zero infrastructure — that constraint forces retail batteries and a vendor cloud. A single operator who can run low-voltage wire is freed from both. Own the wire, delete the battery. Own the stack, delete the subscription.

The two pains, root-caused

Both incumbents are fighting physics and their own business model.

Pain 01 — Battery
~1–2 yr

Nokē runs a coin cell / proprietary 3.6 V lithium that needs a removal tool; cold units crater capacity. Their "fix" was a bigger cell — they moved the field labor, they didn't remove it. Root cause: onboard chemical energy storage. Remove the store, remove the problem.

Pain 02 — Subscription
3 clouds

Nokē (~$5.99/tenant/mo tech fee), DaVinci (software sub added after operators bought the locks), Storage Defender (per-tenant). You're renting three vendors' clouds and your own data. Root cause: you don't own the stack.

Competitive landscape

Same problem, opposite hardware — and an empty quadrant.

Nokē protects an electronic BLE padlock and its mechanism. DaVinci protects a software model for mechanical combination overlocks. Storage Defender sells sensing alongside both. Nobody owns the facility-wired, battery-free, owned-and-sensored quadrant.

The design · power architecture

Wire the corridor, buffer the door, delete the cell.

A per-corridor NEC Class 2, 24 VDC bus (touch-safe, no conduit → cheap retrofit) feeds each door. A supercapacitor / lithium-ion-capacitor buffer at every node supplies the brief motor surge and rides out dropouts — so one thin pair feeds many doors, and there is no service battery to ever replace.

Energy math that ends the debate: a lean node needs ~10–15 J/day; fully loaded with always-on motion + sensors, ~50–100 J/day. The wire delivers that trivially. Best-case dark-corridor solar harvest tops out near ~54 J/day and is wildly intermittent. Wire + supercap wins the moment you're allowed to run wire.

Your "charge it while it's locked" question — answered

Roll-up doors · most units

Don't charge anything.

Electronics live on the fixed inside track. Power is a direct wire — nothing crosses the moving door, so there is nothing to charge across a gap. Also sidesteps the third-party charge-when-closed patents entirely.

DEFAULT — cleanest
Swing-door leaves

Wiping gold contacts.

Magnetically-aligned pogo-pin contacts mate when the door closes (SELV 5–12 V, self-wiping, IP-sealed, hard-gold over nickel). This is your charge-while-closed idea, done right — and a lane distinct from the inductive patent.

WHERE NEEDED
Coastal / exposed only

Sealed inductive.

No contacts to corrode, but ~40–80% efficient, alignment-fussy, and steel doors cause eddy losses (needs ferrite). Most patent-encumbered lane (US 9,787,127). Last resort.

LAST RESORT

Sensor integration · the Storage Defender add-on

Because it's wired, one node does what they sell as three products.

Storage Defender splits Smart Unit / Smart Zone / VisualVerify because battery budgets force it. Wired power relaxes that — and a wired, provisioned node reads outside their "no external wiring / no pairing" claims.

Tier 1 · every node
  • Door open/close (reed / Hall) — highest-value signal, nearly free.
  • Lock-vs-door mismatch = pry / forced-entry alarm.
  • MEMS accelerometer — pry, cut, hammer, removal; µA wake-on-motion.
Tier 2 · where it pays
  • Temp + humidity — climate-controlled product, mold/insurance, HVAC verify.
  • In-unit PIR — run it always-on (you have power); place inside the unit, not on the lock.
Tier 3 · satellites
  • Water / flood leak — ground-floor & basement, on the floor not the lock.
  • Camera / visual verify — separate PoE tier; never on the low-power radio.

Phase 2 · Guide Path

The powered node already at every door becomes a navigation layer.

Because power, a controller, an addressable network, and sensors are already at every unit for the lock, wayfinding and presence signaling are a firmware-and-fixture overlay — a few dollars of LED per door, not a new system. No incumbent can match that incremental cost, because none has the powered node.

Navigate me

"This is your unit."

On check-in, corridor panels route the tenant aisle-by-aisle in their color; their door glows green, neighbors stay dark, guidance times out on arrival.

TENANT
The doctor's-office flag

"A tenant is in Aisle 4."

Aisle-level presence — a lit flag + dashboard tile — derived from the door / PIR / tamper events already in the network. Staff glance and know where people are.

STAFF
Something's off

Exceptions on the same lights.

After-hours presence goes amber; approach to a delinquent or flagged unit turns it red and escalates. Loss-prevention on the sensing you already have.

SECURITY

Two rules baked in. ADA: never color alone — every cue pairs with an arrow, unit number, and/or audio, on a color-blind-safe palette. Privacy: presence is signaled aisle-level and non-identifying to floor staff; identity stays in the secured dashboard and the tenant lease. And one cheap Phase-1 move — reserving an LED channel on the Rev A board (~$1) — makes this a fixture-and-firmware add later, not a board respin.

Three valuation levers for the S-1 story: (1) opex → owned asset lifts NOI, which is what a storage REIT trades on; (2) ancillary revenue captured at 100% margin instead of leaking to vendors; (3) owned IP + a proprietary portfolio-wide access/sensor dataset is a real proptech moat and a differentiator to public markets.

Guardrails

Don't skip these.

  • "Internal use" ≠ patent-safe. Making and using a patented invention both infringe — retail or not. Internal-only lowers exposure, not liability. Design around the claims.
  • Self-storage lien law. Remote access denial is governed by state statutes (notice periods, what you may deny, auction rules). A soft overlock is more legally sensitive than a physical one. Counsel per state.
  • Certifications still apply: UL 294 (access control), FCC Part 15 (radio), NEC Class 2 / Art. 725 (the bus), fire-egress + ADA on interior latches.
  • Fail-safe + mechanical override. A dead facility must never trap a paying tenant's goods — engineering and insurance both require it.

Recommended sequence

From concept to quotable.

  • Formal FTO on the three red-flag claims (re-pull actual claim text first): Nokē US 11,098,500; DaVinci US 10,475,115 / 11,663,650.
  • One-corridor prototype at an NCSS site — Class 2 bus + fixed-track nodes + supercap + RS-485/PoE + your access server + door/tamper/temp sensors. Prove battery-free through a power blip.
  • File a provisional on the system combination before showing anyone outside NDA — cheap insurance and a real IP asset for the REIT story.
  • Pilot one facility; measure NOI impact (vendor-fee elimination + ancillary capture) to build the S-1 numbers.
