The problem

The incumbents have two weak bolts. We pull both.

Weak bolt 01 — the battery
~1–2 year life, replaced by hand.

Coin cells and proprietary lithium packs that degrade in unconditioned units and demand a tool and a truck-roll to swap. Nokē made the cell bigger — it moved the labor, it didn't remove it.

Weak bolt 02 — the subscription
Three vendor clouds, forever.

Nokē (~$5.99/tenant/mo), DaVinci (software fee added after you bought the locks), Storage Defender (per-tenant). You rent access to your own doors — and your own data.

Root cause: both are built to sell into any facility assuming zero infrastructure. That single constraint forces the battery and the cloud.

The insight

You can run wire. They can't assume it.

A single operator installing across its own portfolio can put permanent low-voltage wiring in every corridor. That one freedom collapses both problems at once:

  • Own the wire → delete the battery. Power comes from the building, not a cell.
  • Own the stack → delete the subscription. The authority is your server, not a vendor's.
  • Own the node → fold in sensing and wayfinding for a few dollars more.

A touch-safe NEC Class 2 bus feeds every door. A supercapacitor at each node sources the motor surge, so one thin pair feeds many doors — and there's no service battery to ever replace. Million-cycle life, −40 to +65 °C. On roll-up doors the electronics sit on the fixed track, so there's literally nothing to "charge while locked."
